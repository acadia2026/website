## Workshop Description
This program brings together neuroscience, cognitive science, and computational design around a shared question: how do empirical findings on cognition and perception translate into methods that architecture can use? The workshop is comprised in 2 parts - 3 lectures and 3 practical workshops: the morning sessions are lectures presented by leading experts establishing some of the theoretical foundations in this field; the afternoon sessions are practical workshops from three distinct directions: agent-based modeling of spatial cognition, sound as an underexamined register of environmental perception, and AI-supported computational pipelines for capturing soft values in design. Each workshop track produces a short collective output, presented to the ACADIA conference at the end of the day.

Workshop 1 (Arkar Hein): Operationalizing Spatial Cognition in Architecture using Agent Based Modeling
This workshop addresses how empirical findings on spatial cognition and architectural experience translate into design-relevant computational methods. The central question: how do findings on spatial cognition translate into design methods that engage with how the brain processes space? Participants learn to construct an answer the way researchers actually have to: mapping adjacent literatures, declaring assumptions, encoding those assumptions as model parameters, and reading model outputs as conditional predictions rather than findings. Topics covered: The translation problem — from rating-based and behavioral evidence to ABM parameters; working memory and route persistence; attention and landmark salience; arousal and decision noise; individual differences in spatial cognition (sense of direction, mental rotation, spatial working memory capacity); empirical aesthetics findings on architectural environment; the methodological gap between rating and behavior; inference under absence of direct evidence; parameter design from cognitive mechanism; hypothesis articulation and limit declaration.

Workshop 2 (Sofia Boarino): Sound as a Design Driver: Towards Bio-Informed and Perceptually Attuned Environments
This workshop explores the role of sound as a tool for understanding, interpreting, and designing relationships between people, environments, and living systems. Drawing from architecture, sound studies, ecology, and neuroscience, it investigates how sonic and other invisible phenomena shape our relationship with space, perception, and the environment, and how they may inform new approaches to design beyond conventional form-making. Through lectures, discussion, and collaborative exercises, participants explore how listening can reveal hidden environmental dynamics and inspire alternative design methodologies. The workshop opens new questions around sound, perception, bio-informed design, and the future relationship between computational tools, environmental awareness, and spatial practice — examining how invisible phenomena and processes can become meaningful drivers of design inquiry and spatial imagination beyond form.

Workshop 3 (Dr. Michela Turrin & Antonia Sattler): More Than Performance — AI, Architecture, and Human Experience
In computational design, there is currently a gap between advanced computational support for engineering building performance and the lack of comparable computational support for the soft values of architecture. This workshop addresses — through debate and a hands-on example — how novel computational techniques offer an unprecedented opportunity to close this gap, by integrating the human factor into computational methods, including social, cultural, and emotional dimensions of architecture. The workshop is structured in three parts with 1) a short introduction grounded in discussion with practitioners on how the intersection of architecture and neuroscience is discussed through a proposed framework and architectural case studies; 2) hands-on experience with a simple computational workflow that captures a soft value in relation to design scenarios, using an AI-powered pipeline to inform design-relevant assessments, and 3) the group returns to the framework and initial case studies to critically reflect on opportunities, challenges, and future directions in relation to AI.
